Toilet Clogged and Leaking at the Base: A Pico Rivera, CA Homeowner Guide

By Pico Rivera Express Plumbing · Updated October 23, 2026 · 6 min read

Water on the floor around a toilet is usually one of four things. A clog that pushes water out, a failed wax ring, a loose bolt or a supply line leak, and sometimes just condensation.

Stop the water first, then work out where it comes from

Close the small supply valve at the wall by turning it clockwise, and stop flushing. If the bowl is full and rising, do not keep trying. Mop up and put a towel at the base so you can see whether new water appears.

A leak at the base that appears only when you flush points to the wax ring or the bolts. A leak that shows up all the time points to the supply line, the tank or condensation. If you cannot tell, call (562) 488-9614 and our dispatch can book a visit.

Match the puddle to the cause

Check when the water appears and where it sits. That alone narrows the list.

What you noticeMost likely causeFix
Water only after flushingFailed wax ring or loose closet boltsReset the toilet with a new ring
Water appears with a clogged bowlBlockage backing up through the baseClear the clog, then recheck
Constant drip at the wallSupply line or shutoff valveTighten or replace the supply line or valve
Beads of water on the tank, dry at the baseCondensationDry it and watch, often seasonal
Toilet rocks on the floorLoose bolts or uneven floorReset with shims or tighten bolts
Drips from the tank boltsTank-to-bowl gasket or boltsReplace the gasket set

A slow, steady puddle over days is a repair, not a mop job.

A careful way to test it yourself

You need paper towels, a flashlight and about 15 minutes. Do not unscrew anything yet.

  1. 1

    Dry everything

    Wipe the base, the supply line, the shutoff valve and the tank. Lay dry paper towels around the base.

  2. 2

    Check the supply line

    Hold a dry tissue to each fitting on the supply line and valve. A damp tissue tells you which fitting weeps.

  3. 3

    Look for condensation

    Cold water in the tank can make the outside of the tank wet on a humid day. If the tank is wet but the base stays dry, that may be the whole story.

  4. 4

    Flush once and watch the base

    Flush and watch for water seeping out at the floor line. If the paper towels get wet, the seal is failing. If the toilet rocks at the same time, the bolts are loose.

Why a clog and a leak show up together

A clogged toilet and a leaking base often have the same root cause.

Backed-up water finds the weakest seal

A blocked drain pushes water up and out, and the wax ring is often the weak point. After the clog is cleared, the base may stay dry or it may keep weeping.

Repeated plunging loosens things

Pressure from plunging can rock the bowl and break the seal. If the toilet shifts, it needs to be reset.

Old rings dry out

A ring that has dried or flattened over time may only leak once pressure builds up in a clog.

A clog that comes back signals a deeper problem

If clogs repeat, the issue may be farther down the line. Our Drain Cleaning crews can check.

When to replace the wax ring

Replacing a wax ring means pulling the toilet. Here is when it is worth doing.

  • ✓Water appears at the base after every flush.
  • ✓The floor around the toilet feels soft or looks stained.
  • ✓The toilet rocks even after tightening the bolts gently.
  • ✓A sewage smell lingers near the toilet.
  • ✓The toilet was just removed for floor work or a new flange.
  • ✓Dye in the tank shows color in the bowl, which is a separate flapper leak.

The EPA dye test needs only food coloring in the tank and a 10-minute wait. A positive result means the flapper leaks, not the base. Our Toilet Repair crews handle both, and you can reach them at (562) 488-9614.

When the floor is already damaged

If the subfloor around the toilet is soft or the flange is cracked, a new ring alone will not solve it. A damaged flange or floor needs repair before the toilet is set again. Ask for a written scope and a clear price before work starts.

Homes in Montebello and Commerce built in the 1950s and 60s may have had bathroom updates over the years, so the flange or floor can differ from room to room.
