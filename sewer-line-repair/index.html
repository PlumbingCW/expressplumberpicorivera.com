Sewage is backing up right now

If wastewater is coming up in the lowest drain in the house, treat it as an emergency. Stop running water, skip the washing machine and dishwasher, and keep children and pets out of the wet area. Our 24/7 dispatch picks up overnight and on weekends, and we book same-day visits whenever the day has room.

If the water has reached outlets or floor-level appliances, leave the power off in that area. Do not pour chemical drain openers into a backed-up line, because they sit in the pipe and make the work harder and less safe for whoever opens it. Which drains back up tells us where the problem sits

Use this before we arrive. It helps us bring the right equipment.

  • ✓Only one sink or tub is slow. That is usually a branch clog, and our Sewer Line Cleaning page explains when a main line is involved instead.
  • ✓The lowest tub, shower or floor drain gurgles or fills when you flush a toilet or run the washer. That points to the main line.
  • ✓More than one fixture is affected at the same time, and clearing one does not help the others.
  • ✓Sewage smell outside near a cleanout, or damp ground along the path of the line.
  • ✓A line that clears for a few weeks and then backs up again. Repeat backups are the signal to look inside the pipe rather than clear it again.
  • ✓Tell us if your property has a septic tank. That changes what we check, so mention it when you call us.

Lining, spot repair or full replacement

There are three broad choices. A spot repair fixes one short failed section. Pipe lining, often called trenchless, builds a new liner inside the existing pipe. Replacement removes the old run and installs new pipe, either by digging or by a pull method where the layout allows.

Trenchless sounds like the clean answer, and sometimes it is, but it has limits. It needs a pipe that is still open enough to line, it does not cure a belly where waste pools, and a badly collapsed section may still need digging. We tell you which of those applies to your line before you decide.
