Plumbing trouble follows the work calendar

Commerce has a commercial and industrial base, and the homes that sit among it are residential pockets, not a single neighborhood. Different buildings fail at different times. A shop that closes for a long weekend can return to a flooded back room, and a unit that sat idle can have a heater that will not relight.

Before a busy stretch, a short plumbing check costs less than a shutdown. After a quiet one, check the floor and the heater before the first shift. If you find water, the 24/7 line at (562) 488-9614 is how you get a crew moving.

A reopening check after a long weekend or idle stretch

Ten minutes at the start of the day can catch a slow leak.

  1. 1

    Walk the floors

    Look near restrooms, break rooms and the water heater for wet spots, stains or a musty smell.

  2. 2

    Check the meter

    Read it, wait a couple of hours with nothing running, and read it again. Any movement points to a leak somewhere.

  3. 3

    Run each drain

    Flush toilets and run sinks and floor drains. Slow or gurgling drains suggest a partial blockage building toward a backup.

  4. 4

    Look at the heater

    Check for drips at the base and for a pilot that has gone out. A relight that fails twice calls for a technician.

Commerce water and heater scale

Cal Water's 2025 report for the City of Commerce water system says the company has served the area since 1985 and that supply is local groundwater plus purchased Metropolitan Water District water. Groundwater hardness ran 140 to 155 ppm, with an average of 146.

On Cal Water's scale, 75 to 150 is moderately hard and 150 to 300 is hard, so Commerce groundwater sits right at the boundary. Cal Water notes hard water can build up in pipes and water heaters, which is the reason to ask about a flush schedule on a tank that runs all day. See Water Heater Repair if yours is losing output.

Before we arrive: a burst pipe or active leak in a Commerce building

Four actions for the minutes before the crew pulls up.

Isolate the water

Shut the main or the nearest branch valve. For a leased unit, ask the manager where the master valve is.

De-energize wet gear

Turn off breakers feeding equipment and outlets near the water. Do not touch them while standing in it.

Protect what you can

Pull inventory, tools and electronics off the floor and away from the wet path.

Phone the 24/7 line

Tell dispatch it is an active leak and whether it is a business or a home. Use the emergency number at any hour.
