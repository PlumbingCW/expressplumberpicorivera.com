Water Softener and Filtration

Why older Pico Rivera plumbing fails at odd hours

According to the Census Bureau's 2024 American Community Survey, the median Pico Rivera home was built in 1957. About 86 percent of the city's 17,352 housing units were built before 1980, and the largest single decade is the 1950s. Pipes, valves and water heaters in houses that age have usually been repaired or replaced at least once, and the next failure tends to arrive without warning.

What that means for you: a plumber who treats an emergency call as a diagnosis problem, not just a patch job, saves money. We look at the age and condition of the line around the failure so you know whether a repair will hold or whether the neighboring section is about to go too.

Hard water and your pipes

The City of Pico Rivera's 2023 water quality report lists total hardness at an average of 174.4 mg/L, which is about 10.2 grains per gallon. On the hardness scale Cal Water publishes, that falls in the hard range. Hard water leaves scale on heating elements and inside water heater tanks.

Plan on a tank flush or a tankless descale schedule, and ask us about a water softener if scale keeps coming back. Not sure which utility serves your address? The city's own page has an address lookup, and you can read about it on our water softener and filtration page.

What to do in the first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

These steps limit damage while a plumber is on the way.

  • ✓Shut off the main water valve if water is escaping and you cannot stop it at the fixture.
  • ✓Turn off power to any room with standing water near outlets or appliances.
  • ✓Stop running drains and flushing toilets if you suspect a sewer backup.
  • ✓Move rugs, electronics and paper away from the water.
  • ✓Take a few photos for your records, then call the 24/7 emergency line.

If you smell natural gas, leave the building first and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from a safe place. Call 911 if anyone is in danger.
