Think water is leaking under the floor right now? Do this first

A floor that is hot, wet, or humming with water while every tap is closed is an urgent problem. Our 24/7 dispatch takes slab leak calls overnight and on weekends, and same-day visits are often possible depending on how the day is booked. The number is (562) 488-9614.

While you wait, shut the main water valve at the house, switch off the water heater, and keep cords and floor-level electronics away from damp areas. Do not drill, chisel or pry up flooring to hunt for the source. Tell us what you saw and heard so the right tools are on the truck.

Symptoms that point to water under the floor

No single sign proves a slab leak. Several together are a good reason to call.

  • ✓A warm patch on the floor, especially in a room with no heating vent nearby. A leaking hot-water line can warm the surface above it.
  • ✓The sound of water running or hissing when every fixture is off. Listen with your ear near the floor in the quiet of the night.
  • ✓A water bill that keeps climbing with no change in who lives in the house. EPA says a family of four above 12,000 gallons a month in a colder month may have a serious leak.
  • ✓A meter that keeps moving. Shut everything off for two hours and read it before and after, because any change means a leak somewhere. Then call us with the two readings.
  • ✓Damp carpet, lifting laminate, or a musty smell along the base of a wall.
  • ✓Hot water that takes longer to arrive, or a water heater that seems to run more than it used to.

Jot down the rooms and times you noticed each sign. That short note saves real time on the diagnostic visit.

How we find the leak before anyone opens the floor

The goal is one small, well-placed opening instead of a trench across the house.

  1. 1

    Shut-off and meter test

    We isolate the house and watch the meter to confirm the water is leaking and not just running somewhere in plain view.

  2. 2

    Pressure and line check

    We test hot and cold lines separately. That tells us which side is losing water and narrows the search.

  3. 3

    Listen and scan

    Listening equipment picks up the sound of escaping water through the floor. Thermal imaging adds a picture when a hot line is involved.

  4. 4

    Mark and confirm

    We mark the likely spot and cross-check it from a second direction before any cutting is discussed.

  5. 5

    Talk through the options

    You get the finding in plain words, along with the choices below and a clear price before work starts.

Spot repair, reroute or repipe: the honest trade-offs

The right choice depends on what we find, not on what is easiest to sell.

OptionFits whenTrade-off
Spot repairOne clear leak and the rest of the line looks soundOpens the floor in one place. A second leak may show up later on the same line.
RerouteThe line under the floor is the problem and walls or the attic give a new pathKeeps the slab intact, but new pipe runs through walls or ceilings and needs patching.
RepipeSeveral leaks, or repairs that keep repeatingMost work and most disruption, but it ends the guessing on that line.

We lay out all three even when one looks obvious, so the decision stays yours.
