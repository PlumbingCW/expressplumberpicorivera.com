How to Find a Hidden Water Leak With the Meter Test in Pico Rivera, CA

By Pico Rivera Express Plumbing · Updated October 23, 2026 · 7 min read

You can find most hidden leaks with two cheap checks, a two-hour water meter test and a toilet dye test. This guide walks through both, shows what the results mean, and says when it is time for electronic detection.

The short answer for a leak you cannot see

Turn off every tap and appliance, read your water meter, wait two hours without using any water, then read it again. Per the EPA, any movement at all on the meter means a probable leak. Then test each toilet by tinting the tank water with food coloring.

If both checks point to a leak but you cannot see wet drywall, stains or puddles, the leak is hidden in a wall, a slab or a buried line. That is when electronic detection saves you from cutting open the wrong spot. You can reach our 24/7 dispatch at (562) 488-9614 at any hour.

Run the meter test in about ten minutes of work

The test itself takes two hours, but your hands-on time is short. Pick a stretch when nobody is home or everyone is asleep.

  1. 1

    Shut off everything that uses water

    Close all taps, stop the dishwasher and washing machine, and pause any irrigation timer. Make sure nobody flushes.

  2. 2

    Find and open the meter box

    Most meters sit in a ground-level box near the curb or the front of the lot. Lift the lid and wipe the cover so the dial is readable.

  3. 3

    Look at the leak indicator

    Many meters have a small spinning triangle or star. If it turns while the house is fully shut off, water is leaking somewhere on your side of the meter.

  4. 4

    Wait two hours and compare

    Take a photo of the full register now and another after two hours of zero use. Any change in the numbers is the EPA's signal for a probable leak. If nothing moved, your plumbing is likely tight at least under normal pressure.

  5. 5

    Close the main valve and look again

    If the dial moved, shut your main valve and watch the indicator. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps turning, the leak is between the meter and your main valve, which points to a buried line.

Why small leaks are worth chasing

The EPA's Fix a Leak Week page says the average household leak can lose over 9,300 gallons yearly, and nine percent of homes have leaks losing 50 gallons or more per day. Fixing easily corrected leaks can save about 10 percent on a water bill.

The same page gives a few quick yardsticks. One drip a second from a faucet adds up to over 3,000 gallons a year, while a sprinkler leak only 1/32 inch wide can lose about 6,300 gallons in a month. A family of four using more than 12,000 gallons in a colder month may have serious leaks.

Do the toilet dye test properly

Toilets are the most common silent leaker and the easiest to test. The EPA method takes about ten minutes.

  • ✓Lift the tank lid and set it somewhere safe.
  • ✓Tint the tank water with a few drops of food coloring and do not flush.
  • ✓Wait 10 minutes and look in the bowl without touching the handle.
  • ✓If color appears in the bowl, the flapper or fill valve is leaking.
  • ✓Flush right afterward so the dye does not stain the bowl.
  • ✓Repeat for every toilet, since one bad flapper can hide behind a good one.
  • ✓The EPA suggests replacing a flapper at least every five years.

A failed dye test is usually a cheap fix, and our Toilet Repair crews handle it along with running and rocking toilets. If every toilet passes and the meter still moves, call (562) 488-9614 and describe what you saw.

When to stop testing and call for electronic detection

Do-it-yourself checks tell you that you have a leak. They rarely tell you where. These are the situations where gear beats guessing.

Meter moves with the main closed

That points to the buried line between meter and house. Pressure testing and acoustic listening can pin the spot so the trench is short instead of long.

Suspected slab leak

Warm floor patches, a hum behind a wall or damp carpet near an exterior door suggest a pressurized line under concrete. A slab leak needs tracing with listening and thermal tools. Our Slab Leak Repair page explains the repair choices.

Bill jumped and the toilets pass

Irrigation, a leaking hot line or a failing supply valve are common suspects. Our Leak Detection service works through them in order instead of tearing into walls.

If the leak is outside the house

A leak on the service line between the meter and the home often shows up as a soft patch in the lawn or a damp strip along the driveway. Your own meter test already told you it is out there. The repair is a trenching or pipe replacement job, covered on our Water Line Repair page.
