What the City report says about your water

In its 2023 Consumer Confidence Report, the City Water Division says the tap comes from deep local groundwater wells. Hardness averaged 174.4 mg/L and ranged from 121 to 212. Dividing by 17.1 gives roughly 10.2 grains per gallon.

Your own tap may sit at either end of that range, or outside it. Some addresses belong to Pico Water District instead, and the City's provider lookup page shows which utility bills you. Check that before you buy anything.

Two towns, two very different reports

Hardness changes from town to town. These two nearby reports show how far.

Norwalk

The Golden State Water Norwalk system report lists hardness from 137 to 505 mg/L, averaging 310 mg/L, or about 18 grains per gallon. That is in the very hard category on Cal Water's scale.

Commerce

The Cal Water City of Commerce report shows groundwater hardness of 140 to 155 ppm, averaging 146 ppm. That is moderately hard.

Why it matters to you

A softener sized for Norwalk would be oversized in Commerce. Equipment should follow your tap, not your zip code.

If you live or work near Norwalk, we help there too, but test your tap anyway.

Softener, filter, or both

A softener removes hardness minerals through ion exchange. A filter targets other things such as chlorine taste or sediment. They solve different problems, and many homes want only one.

Cal Water's report says hard water may build up in pipes or water heaters. If that worries you, see Tankless Water Heater for how buildup affects a heat exchanger. We make no health claims about any water treatment.

The salt caveat

Cal Water's report notes that some softeners add salt that causes problems at wastewater treatment plants. It also notes they can raise sodium for people on low-sodium diets.

Ask about those trade-offs before you buy, and ask your utility if local rules affect softener discharge. We will not guess at rules we have not read.
