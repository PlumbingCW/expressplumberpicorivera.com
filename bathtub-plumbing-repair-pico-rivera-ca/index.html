Bathtub Plumbing Repair in Pico Rivera Homes

By Pico Rivera Express Plumbing · Updated October 23, 2026 · 7 min read

Most tub problems come from five parts you can name: the drain, the overflow, the diverter, the spout and the valve cartridge. This guide shows which ones a homeowner can fix and which signs point to a leak inside the wall.

Start with what the tub is telling you

A tub that drains slowly, drips from the spout after you shut it off, or sends water out the shower head when you want the spout is almost always a worn part. Those repairs are small compared with the damage an ignored leak can cause. A stained ceiling below the tub, a soft floor or a musty smell are different. Those point to water that is escaping where you cannot see it.

Sort your symptom first. If it matches a part in the next section, fix the part. If it matches a hidden-leak sign, stop using the tub and call a plumber so the wall is not opened up more than it has to be.

The five parts that fail

Know the name of the part and half the repair is done.

Drain and stopperHair and soap film slow the flow, and the stopper linkage can slip so the tub will not hold water. Lifting the stopper out and cleaning the strainer fixes many slow tubs.
Overflow plateThe plate on the side of the tub connects to the drain through a pipe behind the wall. A worn gasket here can leak into the wall every time the tub is filled high.
DiverterThis is the pull-up knob on the spout or the third handle that sends water to the shower head. When it fails, water keeps running out the spout while you shower.
SpoutA loose or dripping spout is often a worn connection at the wall. Some spouts slip on with a set screw under the lip, and others thread on.
Valve cartridgeInside the wall handle is a cartridge that mixes hot and cold. When it wears, the handle drips, will not shut off, or the temperature swings.

Pico Rivera's 1957 housing era and your tub

For a tub, that matters because many valves and drains in homes of that age have been repaired, patched or replaced at some point, and no one remembers where the access is.

If your home is from that era, look for an access panel on the wall behind the tub, often in an adjoining closet, hall or bedroom. A panel means a plumber can work on the valve and drain without cutting tile. No panel means the repair may need an opening, so ask for the price of that opening before work starts.

Signs the water is going somewhere hidden

Check these with the tub and shower off. Any yes is a reason to get a leak check rather than keep patching the visible part.

  • ✓A brown or yellow stain, bubbled paint or peeling wallpaper on the wall or ceiling next to or under the tub.
  • ✓Soft, spongy or discolored flooring around the tub edge or at the bathroom door.
  • ✓A musty smell that returns after you clean and air the room.
  • ✓Cracked or loose tile and grout that keeps crumbling at the same corner.
  • ✓A higher water bill with no change in how the household uses water.
  • ✓The meter still moving when every fixture is off.

The EPA says to check the water meter before and after a two-hour period when no water is being used. If the meter changes at all, you probably have a leak. If it does, call our dispatch line and ask for Leak Detection, which uses listening and thermal tools instead of demolition.

What a homeowner can do without opening a wall

Cleaning the strainer and stopper, tightening a loose spout at its set screw and clearing the overflow plate gasket are all reasonable weekend jobs. Use the right screwdriver and keep a towel in the tub to catch dropped screws.

The valve cartridge is where most people go wrong. You must shut off water at the right place and work through the trim without cracking old tile. If the handle will not come off, the valve body is corroded or you cannot find the shutoff, stop. A plumber doing Faucet Repair can usually swap the cartridge in far less time than the first attempt takes you.

Why the diverter is worth fixing early

Water that runs from the spout during a shower wastes hot water and pressure. It also means the diverter seal is worn, which tends to progress. Replace it before the tub spout drips constantly.
