How to Find and Test Your Main Water Shutoff Valve in Pico Rivera, CA

By Pico Rivera Express Plumbing · Updated October 23, 2026 · 6 min read

When a pipe bursts, the one thing that matters is how fast you can stop the water. Find your main shutoff now, test it gently, label it and tell everyone in the house where it is.

The valve to find before you ever need it

Your home usually has a main shutoff valve where the water line comes in, and a second valve at the meter that belongs to the utility. The house valve is yours to operate. Turning it off cuts water to every fixture inside.

If water is already escaping, close the main valve, switch off power near wet areas and call (562) 488-9614. Dispatch never closes, so a crew can start on the burst while you keep the water off.

Where to look for it

Locations vary by house, so check these spots in order.

  • ✓Along the front wall of the house, near where a hose bib sits.
  • ✓Inside a garage, often on the wall closest to the street.
  • ✓Near the water heater or in a utility closet.
  • ✓In a basement or crawl space if the house has one.
  • ✓Outside in a box near the foundation or the meter, with a small cover.
  • ✓Behind an access panel in a hallway or laundry room.
  • ✓At the meter box, where the utility's valve sits next to a separate valve on your side.

If you cannot find it after a walk around, trace the pipe backward from the water heater or a hose bib toward the street. Still stuck? Call (562) 488-9614 and we can help you locate it during a visit.

Ball valve or gate valve

The handle tells you which kind you have. The type changes how you test it and how likely it is to fail.

If you have a round wheel, treat it gently. A forced old gate valve can break and leave you with no shutoff at all.

How to test and exercise the valve

Do this once a year. It takes five minutes and shows you whether the valve will work when it matters.

  1. 1

    Warn the house and open a faucet

    Open one cold tap at the lowest point of the house, such as an outside hose bib. That way you will see the flow stop when the valve closes.

  2. 2

    Turn the valve slowly

    For a lever, a quarter turn closes it. For a wheel, turn clockwise a bit at a time. Never force it.

  3. 3

    Confirm that the water stops

    The tap should dwindle to a trickle and stop. If it keeps flowing, the valve is not closing fully.

  4. 4

    Open it again and check for drips

    Turn it back all the way open, then wipe around the stem. A wet stem or packing nut means the valve is wearing out.

  5. 5

    Label it

    Hang a bright tag on the valve saying which way is closed. Tell everyone in the house. The best time to learn it is not at 2 a.m.

So ask your utility about the meter valve. The valve at the meter is theirs, and they can tell you who is allowed to operate it. Your house valve is the one to use in an emergency. Our own Burst Pipe Repair crews can work from a closed house valve.

If the valve will not turn

A stuck valve is common after years without use. Do not reach for a pipe wrench.

Try gentle back and forth

Work the handle a few degrees each way to break up deposits. Small motion first, then a little more.

Check for damage

Look for a bent handle, a cracked stem or a leaking packing nut. If any of these show, stop and call.

Do not use a cheater bar

Extra force can snap the stem inside the body and make the leak worse.

Plan a replacement

A valve that will not close is a risk. Replacing it is a short job, and it can be combined with other work such as Plumbing Installation.

Why older homes should check sooner

Census ACS 2024 figures put the median year built in Pico Rivera at 1957, which means plenty of houses may have older valves. Time does not make a valve fail, but years of sitting open do tend to freeze them. A yearly exercise keeps the stem moving.

Nearby towns show a similar mix. Median years built are 1963 in Montebello and 1957 in Commerce. In all of them, a valve that closes cleanly can cut a flood to a puddle.
