Pico Rivera water and your heat exchanger

Pico Rivera's 2023 water quality report gives an average hardness of 174.4 mg/L, ranging from 121 up to 212. In grains per gallon, that is about 10.2. On the Cal Water scale, anything from 150 to 300 ppm counts as hard.

Hard water carries dissolved minerals, and Cal Water's report says it may build up inside pipes or water heaters. A tankless unit heats water through narrow passages, so buildup there matters. That is the reason we treat a flush as routine care, not an upsell.

Test your tap before you pick a schedule

Your water may not match the citywide average. Part of town is served by Pico Water District instead of the City Water Division, so check which one bills you and read that utility's annual report.

You can also buy a simple hardness test strip and test your tap yourself. Bring the result to the visit and we will set the flush interval around it.

Tankless flush and descale, step by step

This is the usual order of a descale visit. Your manufacturer's instructions come first if they differ.

  1. 1

    Shut off and isolate

    We close the gas or power and the water supply, then open the service valves on the unit.

  2. 2

    Connect the flush loop

    A pump and hoses circulate a descaling solution through the heat exchanger.

  3. 3

    Circulate and watch

    We let the solution work, then check the color and clarity of what comes back.

  4. 4

    Rinse and clean the filter

    Fresh water flushes the loop, and the inlet screen gets cleaned.

  5. 5

    Restart and test

    We return the unit to service, check flow and temperature, and note the date for your records.

Thinking about switching to tankless

These are questions we answer on site. We do not assume the answers from a catalog.

Is the gas line big enough?

Tankless burners draw a lot of fuel at once. We measure the run and check the line serving the unit.

Where does the exhaust go?

We look at wall and roof options, clearances and the condition of anything we plan to reuse.

Is there power nearby?

Most units need an outlet for the controls. We confirm one is close and protected.

Will it keep up with your home?

We ask how many fixtures run together, then compare that to the unit's flow ratings.

If the answers point to a tank instead, we say so. Our Water Heater Installation page lays out the tank scope.

Permits for a tankless change out

The City of Pico Rivera has an Express Permit for a residential tankless water heater change out in the same location. The City asks the contractor to supply license and business license details with the application.

Swapping a tank for a tankless unit, or moving the unit, can be a bigger change. Ask Pico Rivera Building and Safety at (562) 942-2000 what applies to your address.
