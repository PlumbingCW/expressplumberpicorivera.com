What Rivera means in Pico Rivera

Rivera began as a farming community on the flat land between the Rio Hondo and the San Gabriel River. A railway ran through it by the late 1800s, and the first Rivera School opened in 1868. When Pico and Rivera joined as one city on January 7, 1958, the new city took both names.

Today the name lives on at public places. The City of Pico Rivera lists Rivera Park at 9530 Shade Ln, and the county's Rivera Library is at 7828 Serapis Ave. El Rancho Unified School District also runs Rivera Academy and Rivera Middle School.

We do not claim exact Rivera boundaries. Sources disagree on whether Rivera was the northern or the southern of the two old communities, and no official neighborhood map turned up. If you are not sure your street counts as Rivera, it makes no difference to a call. Give your street address and the nearest cross street, and the conversation starts from there.

Housing around Rivera, in plain terms

Pico Rivera grew fast after World War II, when farmland gave way to tract homes, schools and churches. The city's housing element puts half of all homes in the 1950s, so a large share of houses across the city are more than 65 years old.

That is a reason to look at the visible parts of the plumbing, not a claim about what is behind your walls. Original shutoff valves, tired faucets and older water heaters are easy to check. What sits inside the walls or under the yard is something only an inspection can show, and no one should guess from the age of a street.
