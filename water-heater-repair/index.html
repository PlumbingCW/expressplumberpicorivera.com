Repair or replace: the call you make in the first few minutes

Most people phone about a heater with the same question. Is this worth fixing? The honest answer depends on three things. Where the water is coming from, how old the tank is, and whether the problem is a cheap part or the tank itself.

A failed thermostat, element or gas control valve is a repair. A tank that is leaking from its body is not, because the steel shell has failed from the inside. We tell you which one you have before any work begins, and we quote a clear price either way.

Neither column is a rule. Together they show where the decision tends to land.

Gas smell near the heater? Leave first, then call

If you smell natural gas, evacuate the home immediately and call SoCalGas from a safe location at 1-800-427-2200. SoCalGas also says to call 911 if the leak may endanger life or you cannot reach them.

Do not flip light switches, use a lighter, or try to relight a pilot. Once SoCalGas has cleared the home, call our dispatch and we can look at the heater.

Water heater leak: shut off and read where it comes from

Close the cold water valve on top of the heater, then turn the power breaker off for an electric unit or the gas control to pilot or off for a gas one. Put a bucket under the drip and a towel across the floor. Do not touch the tank if water is spreading toward outlets.

A drip at the temperature and pressure relief valve discharge pipe can mean high pressure or a worn valve. A drip at the supply connections is often a tightened fitting. Water coming from the bottom of the tank is the pattern that usually ends in replacement. Photograph it so you can describe it clearly on the phone.

No hot water: the usual causes, by heater type

On a gas tank, the pilot or thermocouple is the first suspect. On an electric tank, a tripped breaker or failed heating element is. A lukewarm result usually points to the lower element or heavy sediment, not a dead heater.

The City of Pico Rivera's 2023 Water Consumer Confidence Report puts average tap water hardness at about 10.2 grains per gallon. That is mineral content that can build up in a tank. If your heater is old and rumbles when it heats, ask us about sediment before you spend on small parts.

Gas, electric and hybrid heaters: what repair looks like on each

The repair list changes with the type of heater. This is what we look at first.

Gas tankPilot assembly, thermocouple, gas control valve, venting and combustion air. Anything that touches gas gets extra care, and flue draft is checked before we leave.
Electric tankBreaker, upper and lower thermostats, heating elements and the reset button. Power is isolated first and we confirm it is off with a meter.
Hybrid (heat pump)Water-side parts such as valves, fittings and the anode rod are ours to fix. Heat pump components may need the manufacturer's service route, and we say so up front.
TanklessScale buildup, igniter trouble and sensors. For a tankless unit that keeps failing, Tankless Water Heater covers the options.

What moves the cost of a heater repair

Four things tend to decide the invoice on a tank repair.

Part and availability

A thermostat is a small item. A gas control valve or a branded element costs more and may need ordering.

Fuel type

Gas work involves venting and gas connections. Electric work is mostly parts and wiring checks.

Age of the unit

A repair on an old tank is a judgment call. We tell you if the part cost approaches the value left in the tank.

Access and code items

Tight closets, old shutoffs or missing drain pans add time to a visit.

Before we arrive at the heater

A few minutes of prep means less water damage and a faster diagnosis.
