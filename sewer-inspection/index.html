Three situations where a camera inspection pays off

The first is buying a home. A line that looks fine from the yard can have roots, a sag or a crack, and the video shows it before you sign. The second is a backup that returns after cleaning, which tells you something is wrong in the pipe and not just in the drain.

The third is planning a repair, so the work begins from a known problem. If a backup is happening today, call our 24/7 line first and treat the inspection as the next step. In that case see Sewer Line Cleaning for clearing the blockage.

What happens during the visit

A short, predictable sequence.

  1. 1

    Find access

    We locate a cleanout or another entry point to the line. If there is none, we explain the option before we proceed.

  2. 2

    Feed the camera

    A flexible cable carries a waterproof camera through the pipe while we watch a monitor and record the video.

  3. 3

    Note the findings

    We mark defects, distances and the direction of the line as we go.

  4. 4

    Walk you through it

    You see the video and hear what each finding means for your decision.

What the video can show

A camera reads the pipe from the inside.

Roots

Fine roots entering at joints or heavier masses blocking the bore.

Cracks and breaks

Hairline cracks, fractures or a collapsed section.

Offsets and sags

Joints that no longer line up, or a low spot where water sits.

Buildup

Grease, scale or debris narrowing the pipe.

A clean line

No defects found. That is a useful result too, especially before a purchase.

Locating the line

Many inspections also mark where the pipe runs. The camera head can carry a transmitter, and a locator above ground picks up its signal, so we can mark the path and depth on the surface. That helps when you plan a repair, a trench or a remodel.

Ask us to mark the line before you plant, pave or build anything. Cleaning and repair pages like Sewer Line Repair explain what comes next if the video shows damage.

What a good inspection report should contain

Ask for these before you pay for any inspection.

  • ✓A recorded video of the full run, not a handful of screenshots.
  • ✓Notes on each defect with the distance from the access point.
  • ✓The pipe material and diameter, where the camera can see them.
  • ✓The direction of the run and the point where it ends.
  • ✓A plain recommendation: no action, cleaning or repair, with the reasoning.

If you have questions about the report, call us and we will walk through the video with you.
