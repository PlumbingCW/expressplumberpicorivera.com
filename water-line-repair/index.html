Signs the line to the house is leaking

The line runs underground, so you notice the effects before you see the pipe.

Soggy ground, no sprinklers

A wet patch or sunken spot between the meter and the house, even in dry weather.

Meter moving with everything off

EPA suggests reading the meter before and after two hours of no use. Any change means a leak.

Sudden pressure drop

Pressure that falls in every fixture at once, rather than one tap, points at the supply.

Higher bills

A family of four above 12,000 gallons in a colder month may have a serious leak, according to EPA.

Low water pressure: separating a line problem from a fixture problem

Not every weak shower is a buried leak. Check these first.

  • ✓Is it one fixture or all of them? One tap points to the fixture, the aerator or a valve under the sink.
  • ✓Is hot weaker than cold? That points at the water heater side.
  • ✓Does the meter move with everything off? If yes, a leak is likely on the line.
  • ✓Did it start after other work on the street? Ask your utility whether work is happening nearby.
  • ✓Is the main shutoff fully open? A partly closed valve can mimic a leak.
  • ✓Still unsure? Call dispatch and describe what you see.

Finding the shutoff and the valve at the meter

Knowing these two valves saves time in any water emergency.

  1. 1

    Find your main shutoff

    It is often near where the line enters the house. Turn it fully closed and test a tap to confirm.

  2. 2

    Find the meter box

    It usually sits near the street in a lidded box. Read the dial before you do anything else.

  3. 3

    Do not force the meter valve

    The valve at the meter belongs to the utility, so contact them if you need it operated.

  4. 4

    Tell us what you found

    Meter reading, dial movement and where the ground is wet all help us narrow it down.

Repair or replace the line

A short, clean failure can often be fixed with a spot repair, which keeps digging small. A line with several leaks or one that has been repaired before may call for replacing the run, which costs more now but reduces the chance of coming back.

We locate the leak before digging so the trench goes where it needs to. If the loss is under the house instead, our Slab Leak Repair page covers that case.
