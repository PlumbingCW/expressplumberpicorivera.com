Why repiping comes up so often here

Census ACS 2024 estimates put the median year built in Pico Rivera at 1957. About 14,955 of 17,352 housing units, or 86.2 percent, were built before 1980. A house that old may still carry its original supply pipes, or a patchwork of repairs from different decades.

That does not mean your pipes have failed. It means an honest look at your actual pipe, plus a plan for the rooms you care about, is worth doing before a leak forces the schedule.

Three signs worth acting on

One sign alone is a reason to look closer. Several together point toward a repipe conversation.

  • ✓Discolored water, especially brown or orange after the water sat unused.
  • ✓Low pressure that shows up at several fixtures, not just one aerator.
  • ✓Pinhole leaks or damp spots that return after a patch.
  • ✓Repairs that keep landing on different sections of the same line.

Copper or PEX, and the honest trade-offs

Both are used for residential supply lines. The right pick depends on your house, budget and preferences.

QuestionCopperPEX
How it goes inRigid, soldered or brazed joints, more cutting and fittingFlexible tubing, fewer joints, easier routing through walls
Wall openingsOften more access needed for straight runsOften fewer openings because it bends
Material costUsually higher at the supplierUsually lower at the supplier
Visible runsLooks tidy where pipe stays exposedOften hidden in walls, so we discuss where it stays visible
Your preferenceSome owners want metal throughoutSome want the quieter, flexible option

We explain the options for your house and list the material on the contract. We do not make code claims here. Ask us what applies to your address.

Living in the house during the job

Most families stay home. We schedule the longest water shutoff for a time that suits you, and we put in temporary access where we can. Plan to have water to drink and a place to wash up for part of the day.

Tell us about anyone with medical needs, a home business or a new baby. We will build the schedule around it and write it into the contract.

What a sound repipe contract includes

The California Contractors State License Board gives clear guidance on home improvement contracts.

  1. 1

    Put it in writing

    The contract states the total price, the payment schedule and the materials.

  2. 2

    Spell out the scope

    It lists which rooms are opened, which patched, and who does the finish work.

  3. 3

    Handle changes in writing

    Any change to the job goes on a written change order before work continues.

  4. 4

    Keep the down payment small

    The CSLB says it should never exceed 10 percent of the price or $1,000, whichever is less, with a bond exception.

  5. 5

    Check the license

    Verify any contractor's California license on the CSLB site at cslb.ca.gov.

Where repiping fits with other work

If a single line is the problem, Water Line Repair may be enough. If you plan to remodel, repiping before the walls close is often the efficient order, and Plumbing Installation covers new fixtures and valves.
