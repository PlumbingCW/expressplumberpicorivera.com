Replacing a Gas Water Heater Thermocouple in Pico Rivera

By Pico Rivera Express Plumbing · Updated October 23, 2026 · 6 min read

A bad thermocouple is a small part with a big job, and it is one of the cheaper reasons a gas water heater loses its pilot. This guide puts the gas safety rules first, then walks through the swap and says plainly where a homeowner should stop.

What a thermocouple does and why it fails

A thermocouple is a short probe that sits in the pilot flame. The heat makes a tiny electrical signal that holds the gas valve open. If the probe wears out, gets sooty or sits out of the flame, the signal drops and the valve closes. The pilot goes out and the heater stops making hot water.

Replacing it is a known repair on standing-pilot heaters. Many newer heaters use electronic ignition and a flame sensor instead, so check your label first. If you are not sure which kind you have, ask a plumber before buying a part.

The swap, once the gas is confirmed safe

This applies to a standing-pilot gas tank heater that is cool, with no gas odor in the room.

  1. 1

    Turn the control to OFF and let it cool

    Set the gas control knob to OFF. Close the gas shutoff on the supply line if you can reach it. Wait at least several minutes so nothing is hot.

  2. 2

    Open the access panel

    Remove the outer door and the burner cover. Locate the pilot tube, the thermocouple, which is the thin copper line with a tip in the flame, and the gas control.

  3. 3

    Unthread the old thermocouple

    Back the nut off at the gas control by hand or with a small wrench. Slide the tip out of its bracket by the pilot burner and take the old part out whole.

  4. 4

    Install the new one

    Seat the new tip in the same bracket so it sits in the pilot flame. Thread the nut on by hand, then snug it a quarter turn with a wrench. Do not overtighten.

  5. 5

    Relight by the label

    Reopen the gas shutoff, then follow the lighting instructions printed on the heater. Hold the knob for the time stated, then release. The pilot should stay lit.

  6. 6

    Check the connection

    Brush soapy water on the fitting you touched. Bubbles mean a leak and the gas goes off right away. Also watch the flame for a clean blue color.

Where DIY stops

Be honest with yourself about this list. Any one of these is a reason to hand the job to a pro.

  • ✓You smelled gas, even briefly, before or during the job.
  • ✓The heater is in a closet or garage with poor ventilation, or you see soot and a yellow flame.
  • ✓The pilot will not stay lit after a new thermocouple.
  • ✓The tank leaks at the base, which means replacement, not repair.
  • ✓The label says electronic ignition and you cannot identify the part.
  • ✓You have never worked on gas fittings and feel unsure.

A new thermocouple that fails to hold usually means the gas control valve is the real problem. That is a repair for a plumber and may be a sign that replacement makes sense, so ask us about Water Heater Replacement if the heater is old. Water Heater Repair handles the rest.
