Cast iron drain and sewer pipe can rust from the inside, crack or scale shut over decades. A camera inspection shows which problem you have, and that decides between lining the pipe and replacing it.

Start with the camera, then pick lining or replacement

Pipe lining makes sense when a pipe is still the right shape but is rough, pitted or leaking at small spots. Replacement makes sense when the pipe has collapsed, sagged or broken apart. You cannot tell which from the surface, so a camera look comes first.

If you have repeat backups or a gurgling main line, our 24/7 dispatch at (562) 488-9614 can send a crew to run a camera and show you the footage before anyone suggests a repair.

Signs an old drain line is failing

No single sign proves a pipe is bad. Two or three together are a good reason to look inside.

  • ✓Slow drains in several fixtures at once, not just one sink.
  • ✓A main line that clogs again within weeks of being cleared.
  • ✓Sewage smell in the yard, garage or near a floor drain.
  • ✓Gurgling in tubs or toilets when another fixture drains.
  • ✓Soft, sunken or unusually green patches along the path of the line.
  • ✓Rust-colored water or flakes coming out of a cleanout during cleaning.
  • ✓Cleaning cable that keeps catching at the same spot.

If three or more of these sound familiar, ask for a Sewer Inspection before you spend money on another round of cleaning. Reach us any time at (562) 488-9614.

Lining versus replacement at a glance

Both fix a failing pipe. They differ in how much digging, which conditions they handle and what they leave behind.

QuestionPipe liningReplacement
DiggingUsually small access pointsTrench or tunnel along the pipe
Best conditionRough, pitted or cracked but still alignedCollapsed, offset, bellied or separated
Pipe sizeSlightly smaller inside after liningNew pipe sized to the job
Surface damageLimited to entry pointsLandscape, concrete or flooring may be opened
Needs a clear pathYes, so the pipe must be cleaned firstNot required
Cost driversLength, diameter, cleaning neededLength, depth, surface to restore, connections

Prices depend on the factors listed here, so ask for a written scope that names the length and method.

What a camera visit looks like

The visit is quick and does not need walls opened. This is how it usually goes.

  1. 1

    Locate the cleanout

    The tech finds an access point on the line, often outside near the house or in a garage. If none exists, one may be added.

  2. 2

    Clear what blocks the view

    Grease, roots or scale are cleaned back enough for the camera to pass. Our Sewer Line Cleaning work often happens at this stage.

  3. 3

    Run the camera and record

    The head travels the line while you watch the screen. Cracks, offsets, low spots and roots show up on video.

  4. 4

    Walk through the findings

    You get a plain explanation of what the footage shows and which repairs fit. Nothing starts until you agree on a clear price.

Where lining runs into trouble

Lining depends on a pipe that is still in line. If the camera finds a belly where water sits, a joint that has shifted, or a section that has fallen in, a liner will not cure it. Those spots need digging, and our Sewer Line Repair page covers spot repair and full replacement.

Heavy scale also matters. If the pipe is thick with rust nodules, it must be cleaned out first, sometimes by hydro jetting, before a liner can bond. Ask any contractor how they plan to clean the pipe and what they will do if the camera shows a collapse partway through.

Questions to ask before you approve a liner

Ask for the camera footage, the length being lined, and what happens if a section turns out to be broken. A written scope with the total price and any change orders protects you, and the CSLB recommends that for home improvement contracts. You can also check a contractor's licence on the CSLB site.
