Leaking under the sink right now: before we arrive

Turn the disposal off at the wall switch and then at the breaker, because a unit that is wet at the bottom can short. Close the two shutoffs under the sink if the cabinet is filling. Empty the cabinet, put a bucket under the leak and keep power cords up off the floor.

Do not run the disposal again, and never put your hand in the chamber. Call our after-hours line and tell us where the water appears. We offer same-day visits when a slot is open and pick up overnight when the cabinet is flooding.

If the sink also backs up on the other side, the problem may be the drain below the disposal. Our Drain Cleaning page explains what we do for a kitchen line.

Bottom leak versus sink flange leak

Where the water comes from decides whether we repair or replace.

Leak from the bottom of the unit

A crack in the housing or a failed internal seal means the disposal is finished. Repair is rarely worth it.

Leak at the top flange

The putty or gasket under the sink flange has dried or shifted. We reset it and test, and the disposal stays.

Leak at the discharge pipe

The outlet gasket or the trap connection has worn or loosened. This is usually a small repair.

Leak at the dishwasher inlet

The dishwasher hose connection to the disposal has loosened. We tighten or replace the fitting.

Reset and hex-key unjam, with the power off

Many jams clear without a service call. Follow the order, because the power step is not optional.

  1. 1

    Power off

    Switch off the wall switch and the breaker, or unplug the unit if it has a cord.

  2. 2

    Look, do not reach

    Use a flashlight and long tongs to remove anything you can see. Keep your hands out of the chamber.

  3. 3

    Turn the hex key

    Fit the hex key in the socket at the center of the bottom of the disposal and work it back and forth to free the flywheel.

  4. 4

    Press the reset

    The red reset button on the bottom pops out after an overload. Press it in once the unit has had a moment to cool.

  5. 5

    Test with water

    Restore power, run cold water, then switch on. If it hums again or leaks, stop and call us.

When to replace instead of repair

A disposal with a leak from the body, a motor that trips again after a reset, or a flywheel that binds repeatedly is near the end. The cost of repeated visits can pass the cost of a new unit. We tell you which side your unit is on before we quote.

If you are ready to upgrade, ask about stronger motors and sound insulation. A model that is too small for a busy kitchen will jam sooner, so we match horsepower to how you cook.

What a disposal install covers

A full swap is more than hanging a new unit.

  • ✓Disconnect and remove the old disposal and its mounting ring.
  • ✓Check the sink flange, replace the seal and reuse the flange only if it is sound.
  • ✓Mount the new unit and connect the discharge, trap and dishwasher inlet.
  • ✓Confirm the electrical connection is safe, with the power off while we work.
  • ✓Run water and test for leaks at every joint before we leave.
