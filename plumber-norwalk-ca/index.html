A median build year of 1957 and what it asks of the plumbing

The Census ACS 2024 one-year estimate counts 27,169 housing units in Norwalk, with a median build year of 1957. About 82.3 percent of the housing was built before 1980. In practical terms, most Norwalk houses are old enough that the original supply and drain materials have either been replaced or are due for a hard look.

If your house is one of the unrenovated ones, the useful question is not whether something will fail but which part goes first. The table below sorts that out by era, and the calls we get usually follow the same order.

Three quick checks on your own Norwalk house

These tell you more than the city average does.

  1. 1

    Find the heater label

    The data plate on the tank shows the build date. A unit that is a decade or more old and sits in hard water is a replacement candidate, not a repair candidate.

  2. 2

    Look at the pipe where it enters

    Copper, galvanized steel and plastic look different near the main valve. Dull gray steel with rust streaks points toward the repiping conversation.

  3. 3

    Read the meter

    Turn everything off for two hours and check the meter before and after. Any movement suggests a leak, and EPA recommends a licensed plumber for the follow-up.

Norwalk's hard water and the water heater

The Norwalk water system report from Golden State Water for 2025 describes a blend of treated groundwater from the Central Groundwater Basin and imported water delivered by the Metropolitan Water District. Hardness averaged 310 mg/L, which is about 18 grains per gallon, and ranged from 137 to 505 mg/L. On the Cal Water scale, 300 and above counts as very hard.

Golden State Water does not necessarily supply every Norwalk address, so check which utility bills you. If yours is a hard-water address, scale in a tank is a real factor in how long the heater lasts and when to replace it. Ask us about it when you call (562) 488-9614.
