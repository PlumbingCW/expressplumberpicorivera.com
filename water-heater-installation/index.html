Start with the scope list, not the tank

Most installation surprises come from items nobody wrote down. A tank shows up, then the crew finds the vent is the wrong size or the shutoff valve will not close. The fix is a written scope before the order goes in.

On a planned job we walk the existing setup with you, note what stays and what changes, and put each item on the list. You approve the list and the price together. If the heater has already failed and water is on the floor, Water Heater Replacement is the faster path, and our 24/7 dispatch line is open for that.

What belongs on an installation scope

Ask any plumber to confirm these items in writing. We include them on every quote for a tank install.

  • ✓Tank capacity matched to your household and your busiest hour.
  • ✓Fuel type confirmed, gas or electric, with the supply line or circuit checked.
  • ✓Venting path inspected and sized for the new unit.
  • ✓Expansion tank sized and installed where the system needs one.
  • ✓Temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge line.
  • ✓New shutoff valve on the cold supply and flexible connectors.
  • ✓Drain pan and drain line where the location calls for one.
  • ✓Permit handling, haul-away of the old tank and a startup check.

Gas or electric, and where the exhaust goes

Most tank swaps keep the same fuel, because the gas line or the circuit is already in place. Changing fuel is possible, but it adds a gas line or a new electrical run, and that belongs on the scope as its own line.

Gas units also need a venting path that carries exhaust outdoors. We check the existing vent for slope, connections and condition before we reuse any of it. A new unit does not make an old, damaged vent safe.

Expansion tank and relief valve

An expansion tank absorbs pressure swings in a closed water system. The temperature and pressure relief valve is the safety outlet if something goes wrong. Both are line items on our scope, and we explain why each one is on your list or off it.

Gas smell, and how your tank is secured

Gas smell near the heater means you leave the house and phone SoCalGas on 1-800-427-2200 from outside. If anyone is in danger or the utility will not pick up, dial 911. Call us for the repair only after that.

Ask us to check how your tank is secured. We will tell you what we see on the current setup and note it on the quote, so the new install is mounted the way you expect.

What shapes the price of an install

Here is what moves the number on your quote.

Tank type and capacity

A larger or higher-efficiency tank costs more at the supplier. Smaller homes often need less.

Venting and gas work

A reusable vent keeps the job simple. A reroute or new gas line adds labor and material.

Location and access

Garage, closet, attic and tight utility rooms each change how long the swap takes.

Permit and code updates

Bringing old connections up to a safe standard can add parts to the scope. We list them before you approve.

Permits for a tank install in Pico Rivera

The City of Pico Rivera offers an Express Permit for a residential water heater change out in the same location. That fits most tank-for-tank swaps. The contractor supplies license details with the application.

Moving the heater, changing the fuel or adding one where none existed is a different job. For anything outside the same-location change out, ask Building and Safety at City Hall, 6615 Passons Blvd, (562) 942-2000. We will tell you what we know, and the City has the final answer.

Install day, in the order it happens

A typical same-location swap follows this order.

  1. 1

    Walk-through and shutoffs

    We confirm the scope, find the shutoffs and protect the floor.

  2. 2

    Drain and remove

    The old tank is drained, disconnected and carried out.

  3. 3

    Set and connect

    The new unit is placed, connected to water, gas or power, and vented.

  4. 4

    Safety fittings

    Expansion tank, relief valve and discharge line go in as listed.

  5. 5

    Startup and review

    We fire it up, check for leaks and show you the shutoff and temperature setting.
