Galvanized Steel Pipe in Pico Rivera Homes

By Pico Rivera Express Plumbing · Updated October 23, 2026 · 6 min read

Galvanized pipe is zinc-coated steel that was common in older houses, and it wears from the inside out. This guide helps you identify it, read the warning signs and weigh your repiping options without being rushed.

How to tell if your home has galvanized pipe

Galvanized pipe looks dull gray, has threaded joints and often shows rust streaks or crusty bumps on the outside. A magnet sticks to it, which separates it from copper and plastic. Scratch a small spot with a screwdriver. Silver-gray metal under the surface and a magnet pull points to galvanized steel.

Look where pipes enter the house, near the water heater and under sinks. A quick check costs nothing, and the answer decides whether the rest of this guide applies to you. If you are unsure, have a plumber confirm it during any visit.

Local note

Census housing-era figures and what they suggest for your pipes

Census American Community Survey 2024 five-year data counts 17,352 housing units in Pico Rivera. Of those, 14,955, or 86.2 percent, were built before 1980. That includes 1,993 units from the 1940s and 935 from 1939 or earlier.

Those counts say how old the housing is, not what pipe is inside any one house. They do tell you that many homes here are old enough that galvanized supply lines were once a normal choice, and that some have since been replaced in whole or in part. Treat the era as a prompt to check, and treat a mixed system, where new copper meets old steel, as a real possibility in your wall.

Warning signs that the pipes are wearing out

One sign is a note to watch. Two or more is a reason to book an inspection.

Weak water pressure

Mineral scale and rust narrow the inside of the pipe over time, so showers and faucets slow down at the far end of the house.

Brown or orange water

Rust-colored water that clears after a minute may be pipe rust. Water that stays discolored needs a look at the heater and the pipes.

Leaks at threads or joints

Galvanized pipe fails first at the threaded fittings, where the zinc coating was thinnest. Pinhole leaks and damp spots near a fitting are the classic pattern.

Flaking or crusty deposits

Orange rust, white scale or green-tinged crust on the outside of a pipe show the metal is breaking down.

Repairs that keep coming back

A fix that holds for months and then a leak appears a few feet away means the whole run is tired, not one spot.

How a careful repipe quote should go

You should see these steps in order, and you should never be asked to decide on the spot.

  1. 1

    Find the pipe type in each run

    The plumber traces supply lines from the meter to the house, to the heater and to each bath and kitchen, and notes what each run is made of.

  2. 2

    Look for the failing points

    A visual check of fittings, wall access and fixture pressure shows which sections are worst and where leaks are most likely.

  3. 3

    Compare options in writing

    You get a written scope covering materials, wall access, patching and the price, before work starts. The California contractor board advises that contracts be in writing with the total price and payment schedule.

  4. 4

    Confirm permits with the City

    Permit rules vary, so ask the Pico Rivera Building and Safety division what applies to your job. Your contractor should know the process and hold the right licence.

How long does it last and what are the options

There is no honest single number for how many years galvanized pipe lasts. It depends on the water, the pipe wall, how it was installed and whether it has been partly replaced. Pipes that look fine outside can be narrow inside, which is why age alone does not tell the story.

Options range from replacing the worst runs to running new supply lines through the whole house. We explain each in plain terms during a visit, and our Repiping service covers both. If you only have a leak so far, Leak Detection can locate it without opening more wall than needed.

What to do this week, before anyone digs

These checks take under an hour and give a plumber a head start.

  • ✓Run each faucet one at a time and note which ones have weak flow.
  • ✓Photograph any rust streaks, damp spots or crusty joints you can see.
  • ✓Find your main shutoff valve and make sure the handle turns.
  • ✓Write down when the brown water shows up, such as first thing in the morning.
  • ✓Ask your utility for its annual water quality report so you know what is in the water coming into the pipes.

Bring the notes to the visit. They shorten the diagnosis and keep the quote honest.
