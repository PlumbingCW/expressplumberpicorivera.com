Why Your Water Bill Jumped: Common Causes in Pico Rivera, CA Homes

By Pico Rivera Express Plumbing · Updated October 23, 2026 · 6 min read

A sudden jump in a water bill almost always comes from one of five places. Toilets, irrigation, hidden leaks, a change in household use or a billing error. You can sort most of them out with a meter reading.

Read the meter before you guess

Compare your bill with the last few months, then go read the meter. Turn everything off, read the dial, and read it again two hours later. The EPA's rule of thumb is simple. Any change on the dial means a probable leak.

If the meter stays still, look at usage and irrigation next. If it moves, a hidden leak is likely, and our team can trace it. Dispatch is open 24/7 if you want to talk it through, just use the emergency line.

Local note

Pico Rivera water and housing: what it means for you

Two different utilities send water bills to Pico Rivera homes. The city's page for finding your water service provider says the City Water Division covers the city-served area, with its office on Passons Blvd and a line at (562) 801-4316. Addresses outside that area belong to Pico Water District on S. Church St, at 562-692-3756. An address lookup map on the page shows City in green and Pico Water District in red.

Confirm who sends your bill before you call anyone, since meter readings, billing disputes and any adjustment policy are theirs to explain. Once you know, ask them for your usage history, because the EPA treats a family of four passing 12,000 gallons in a month, in cooler weather, as a likely serious leak. If your numbers are near or above that, move straight to the meter test.

A weekend plan to find the cause

You can do this in order and stop when you find it.

  1. 1

    Pull the last three bills

    Note usage per billing period, not just the amount. A sudden step up points to a new leak or a new habit. A slow climb may be a worsening drip. Write down the billing dates too, because a longer cycle can make a bill look bigger than it is.

  2. 2

    Do the two-hour meter test

    Make sure everything is off, including irrigation and the ice maker. Record the register before and after.

  3. 3

    Dye test every toilet

    Add a few drops of food coloring to each tank and wait 10 minutes. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking. Our Toilet Repair team can replace it.

  4. 4

    Walk the yard

    Run each irrigation zone and look for soggy ground, geysers or lawn patches that stay wet. Valve boxes and sprinkler heads are the usual weak spots, and a stuck valve can keep a zone running long after the timer says it stopped.

  5. 5

    Close the main and watch the dial

    If the dial keeps turning with the main closed, the leak is between the meter and the house. Book Leak Detection to find the spot.

Where a hidden leak hides

If the toilets are fine and the irrigation is off, these are the usual suspects.

Under the slab

Pressurized lines under concrete can leak for months. Warm floors and the sound of running water are clues. See our Slab Leak Repair page.

Between meter and house

The service line is buried and out of sight. Wet patches in the yard or a driveway edge can reveal it.

In a wall

A pinhole in a supply line or a failing valve can leak behind drywall until the paint bubbles or a baseboard swells. Small, steady wetness is the pattern to look for.

At the water heater

Tank fittings and the relief valve can drip slowly into a pan or drain and go unnoticed. A look at the pan and the floor below the tank takes a minute. See Water Heater Repair if it is wet.

When the meter says it is not a leak

If the meter is still and the toilets pass, the bill may reflect actual use. Guests, a new pool or a hot spell with more irrigation all raise it. Compare the billing dates to see whether the cycle was longer than usual, and ask whether the reading was actual or estimated.

If the numbers still do not add up, ask your utility to confirm the reading and check the meter. Comparing bills with a friend in Norwalk or Montebello will not tell you much, since towns around Pico Rivera have their own water systems and rates.
