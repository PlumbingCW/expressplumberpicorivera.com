Finding Rio Vista on the map

Rio Vista is a Spanish name that means river view. In Pico Rivera it is tied to two public places on the same road: Rio Vista Park and Rio Vista Elementary, both on Coffman-Pico Road. Those are the anchors this page uses.

We do not draw a boundary around Rio Vista. No official neighborhood map was found, and a directory listing is not the same thing as a city record. If your street is somewhere near Coffman-Pico Road, you can mention that on the call. A street address and a cross street are what matter in practice.

Three checks before you call about a leak or a high bill

These take a few minutes and make the call more useful.

  1. 1

    Read the meter with everything off

    Close every tap and appliance that uses water. If the meter dial still moves, water is going somewhere. The page on Leak Detection describes the meter test in full.

  2. 2

    Check the toilets and the heater

    A toilet that runs on can waste a lot of water without a sound. Look under the water heater for dampness too. Water Heater Repair explains what a leaking tank means.

  3. 3

    Look outside

    Walk the yard and look for soggy ground, a wet spot near the meter box or irrigation that runs when it should not. A damp patch on the house side of the meter is the homeowner's problem, and Water Line Repair covers it.

Who owns what: the utility or you

Knowing the dividing line saves a wasted call.

The meter and the valve beside itThese belong to your water provider. Ask that office if the dial or valve behaves oddly.
The line from the meter to the houseThis is generally the property owner's side. A wet lawn between the meter and the house points here.
Pipes, fixtures and the heater insideThese are yours. A plumber can look at any of them, and so can a landlord if you rent.
Your sewer line to the streetAsk your provider or the city which office handles the connection before you assume a clog is yours alone.

An aging housing stock and what it means for you

Pico Rivera's general plan calls its housing stable yet aging and says about 87 percent of homes are more than 30 years old. That figure is dated, and it covers the whole city.

The plain reading is that wear is normal and worth watching. A faucet that drips, a valve that will not close fully and a heater past its prime are all ordinary things in homes of that age. Do not assume anything about the pipes behind a wall until someone has looked.

Local note

Check the provider before a billing call

The city runs an address lookup that marks which of the two providers covers a street, so ten seconds on that page saves a call to the wrong office. Do this before asking anyone about pressure, a meter or a bill.
